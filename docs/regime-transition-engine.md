# Regime transition analytics (MCE-11)

`RegimeTransitionAnalyticsEngine` describes the observed history of MCE-10 classifications. It does not classify again, estimate a future state, or produce a trading signal. `RegimeHistory.build(...)` converts full MCE-10 states or lightweight `RegimeHistoryPoint` records into an immutable, timestamp-sorted index. Duplicate observation timestamps are rejected; out-of-order input is sorted. Every accepted observation must have become available by the query clock. The causal query ignores future observations and future availability, and rejects mixed classifier fingerprints among the accepted points. Building the history once avoids repeated ingestion work. There is no process-global history or mutable cache.

All results have an explicit `as_of_ns`. The default windows are the latest 1, 5, 20, and 60 **XNYS trading sessions**, including the current session after its regular open. The 20-session window is the primary window for matrix, dwell, dimension, and stability summaries. A holiday or weekend query uses the latest prior session. A transition at the window boundary is included if its new observation is at that boundary, even when the prior observation precedes it. The history can be queried at any earlier clock without changing prior results.

## Definitions and units

| Measure | Exact definition | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline transition | Adjacent accepted observations have different headline labels. Identical repeats are not transitions. `UNKNOWN`, `MIXED`, and `TRANSITIONAL` are counted like every other label. | Count is zero when observed history has no changes. |
| Current age | `age_ns = as_of_ns − first observation timestamp in the current uninterrupted headline run`; trading nanoseconds count only regular XNYS time. `age_sessions` counts XNYS trading dates from run start through query date, inclusive; `age_observations` counts records, with no assumed sampling cadence. | `None` without a current observation. Current age is right-censored and remains separate from completed episodes. |
| Completed dwell | For each headline run that both starts and ends inside the primary window, duration is the regular-session time from its first observation to the first different observation. Session span counts trading dates from start through transition date, inclusive. | A left-censored run, the ongoing run, and a run containing a gap above the configured limit are excluded. Counts remain available; mean, median, min, max session span and mean trading nanoseconds need at least two completed episodes per label by default. |
| Observed transition frequency | For source `A` and destination `B`, `count(A→B) / all observed exits from A` in the primary window. Self-transitions are absent. | Each row with exits sums to one; regimes with no observed exits have no row. This is a retrospective conditional frequency, **not** a forecast probability. |
| Occupancy | Assign each observed interval `[timestamp_i, timestamp_(i+1))` to the last observed headline, intersect it with the selected window and regular XNYS hours, and divide each label's observed trading nanoseconds by total covered trading nanoseconds. The final observation extends only to the query clock. | `None`/empty when there is no covered trading time. Fractions sum to one when present. `UNKNOWN`, `MIXED`, and `TRANSITIONAL` retain their own shares. No snapshot-count weighting. |
| Transition rate | Headline transition count divided by **covered session equivalents**. One full regular session equals one equivalent, including an early close; partial sessions contribute their elapsed fraction. | `None` when covered session equivalents are zero. Unit: transitions per observed trading session. |
| Churn | `min(1, transition_rate / churn_saturation_per_session)`, default saturation 2 transitions/session. | `None` without a rate; `[0,1]` otherwise. This time normalization avoids equating minute and daily observation counts. |
| Reversal and oscillation | A reversal is adjacent headline transitions `A→B`, then `B→A`, with no more than 2 trading-session equivalents between them by default. `reversal_fraction = reversal_count / (transition_count − 1)`; oscillation score equals this fraction. | Fraction/score `None` with fewer than two headline transitions; otherwise `[0,1]`. |
| Dominant pair | Count both directions of each unordered headline pair in the primary window. Highest count wins; alphabetical label order breaks ties. | `None` with no headline transitions. |
| Dimension change | Compare each of nine labels on adjacent accepted observations: direction, trend, volatility, liquidity, correlation, breadth, dispersion, stress, sector rotation. Rate is changes per covered session equivalent; age is trading dates since that dimension last changed. | A dimension can move while the headline stays fixed. Each dimension's `stability_score = 1 − min(1, dimension_rate / saturation_rate)` when quality is sufficient. |
| Synchronization | Headline transitions with at least one simultaneous dimension change divided by all headline transitions. Each dimension also reports its own simultaneous-change count/fraction. `co_transition_count` counts all observation steps with at least two dimension changes, including unchanged headlines. | Fractions `None` with no headline transitions. |
| Intensity | `0.5 × headline_churn + 0.5 × dimension_churn`; dimension churn is the bounded rate of all dimension changes divided by nine dimensions and the same saturation rate. | `[0,1]` with adequate history/quality; otherwise `None`. No invented headline-distance geometry. |
| Directed transition intensity | For each observed ordered pair `A→B`, average `number of dimensions changed at that transition / 9`. Each recent transition also carries its own changed-dimension fraction. | `[0,1]` for pairs with at least one observed transition; absent pairs have no row. `A→B` and `B→A` stay separate. |
| Stability | `.35 × min(1, current_age_sessions / primary_window_sessions) + .30 × (1 − headline_churn) + .20 × (1 − dimension_churn) + .15 × (1 − oscillation_score)`; an unavailable oscillation score contributes its no-observed-oscillation value of 1 only after the history quality gate passes. `instability = 1 − stability`. | `[0,1]` when quality gates pass; otherwise `None`. Typed contributors reconcile exactly with the score. |

The MCE-10 `evidence_strength` measures internal support for one classification. MCE-11 `stability_score` measures **temporal persistence** of observed labels. Neither is a calibrated probability.

## Coverage, gaps, and quality

An observation interval whose elapsed **regular-session** time exceeds `maximum_gap_trading_ns` (default 13 hours) contributes no occupancy or rate denominator. It raises `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOW_COVERAGE`. `time_coverage = covered regular-session nanoseconds / regular-session nanoseconds in the window up to the query clock`. This is based on timestamps, including early closes, and does not assume uniform sample spacing. The raw transition count remains observable even when timing coverage is poor.

The default stability/intensity gate requires at least five primary-window observations, two observed XNYS sessions, at least 50% time coverage, no more than 50% `UNKNOWN` observations, and at least 50% observations with `GOOD` or `DEGRADED` MCE-10 quality. Existing point quality flags propagate. A failing gate returns `None` for stability, instability, intensity, and dimension stability rather than a fabricated zero. Count, sequence, frequency, occupancy, and quality diagnostics remain inspectable. Dwell summaries independently require two completed episodes per label. A large gap can make the current observed age an upper bound on continuously observed persistence; the gap flag and coverage must be read with it.

## Configuration and snapshot

`features.regime_transition` holds enablement, positive sorted window lengths, a primary window drawn from them, history/quality thresholds, maximum gap, reversal horizon, saturation rate, recent sequence limit, and nonnegative stability weights summing to one. All result-relevant fields enter the configuration fingerprint. The typed result records both its analytics fingerprint and the accepted MCE-10 classifier-history fingerprint. MCE-10 regime labels and rules are unchanged.

`apply_regime_transition_to_snapshot` adds `regime_transition.*` values to schema-1.0 `feature_values` and preserves the existing MCE-10 headline, categorical dimensions, confidence, and snapshot quality. Stable names include `regime_transition.age_sessions`, `.age_ns`, `.age_trading_ns`, `.count.<N>d`, `.rate.<N>d`, `.churn.<N>d`, `.rate`, `.churn`, `.stability`, `.instability`, `.oscillation`, `.intensity`, `.synchronized_fraction`, and `.dimension.<name>.count/.rate`. `<N>d` means N **trading sessions**, not calendar days. Unavailable scalars are omitted. Typed results retain the bounded transition sequence, full observed matrix, dwell summaries, occupancy, dimensions, and quality flags.

The dependency graph remains causal observations → MCE-3–MCE-9 features → MCE-10 deterministic regimes → MCE-11 descriptive transition analytics. No upstream engine depends on MCE-11. Historical exit frequencies do not estimate what regime comes next. Statistical and latent-state models remain deferred to MCE-12.
